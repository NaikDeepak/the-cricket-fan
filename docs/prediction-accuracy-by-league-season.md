> **Correction (added after further review — read before trusting the analysis below):**
> The "pre-2025" column in the summary table is **in-sample** — the committed
> model trains on `years <= 2024`, so 2007-2024 backtest numbers reflect the
> model scoring data it trained on, not real predictive skill. The "Δ Model"
> column therefore compares in-sample accuracy to genuinely out-of-sample
> accuracy, per league. That's a confound: leagues with the deepest match
> history (IPL, PSL, BBL, CPL) show the biggest apparent "drop" mostly
> because they had the most in-sample data to memorize, not necessarily
> because of squad turnover. A controlled test (within-season demeaned
> correlation between a computed turnover metric and per-match backtest
> correctness, n=126 IPL team-seasons) came back **null** (r=-0.056,
> p=0.54), with a direct counterexample: IPL 2026 has *lower* measured
> turnover than 2025 (0.26 vs 0.57) but *worse* model accuracy (53% vs 60%).
> **The "this is the evidence for the squad-turnover feature" framing below
> is retracted pending an honest out-of-sample-vs-out-of-sample comparison.**
> The confirmed, measured cause is the calendar-year train/val/test split
> (see below) — that one has a real experiment behind it (rolling-split
> retrain recovers accuracy in some leagues). Squad turnover remains a
> plausible follow-up hypothesis, not a established finding.

## Why this report exists

User observation: IPL accuracy was 80s-high-80s through 2024, then dropped
to the 50s-60s in 2025/2026. Question: is this bad 2025/2026 data
ingestion, or something else? This report checks whether the same pattern
shows up **outside IPL** — it does, almost everywhere, which rules out an
IPL-specific ingestion bug.

## Root cause

`bot/train.py:88-90` splits by year **globally across all 28 leagues
combined**, not per league:

```python
tr = years <= max_year - 2   # train: everything through 2024
va = years == max_year - 1   # 2025 — used ONLY to fit the isotonic calibrator, LightGBM never trains on it
te = years == max_year       # 2026 — true blind holdout
```

So the committed model has **never trained on a single 2025 or 2026 match,
in any league.** 2025/2026 backtest numbers are out-of-sample generalization
by construction, not a data quality problem — confirmed by the fact the
data itself (venue/overs/runs/pp/death columns) has zero nulls for any IPL
season 2007-2026.

## Two things going on, not one

**1. Universal effect — the model's holdout gap.** Elo (`Δ Elo` column) needs
no training; it's a continuously-updated chronological rating replayed over
every match. It stays roughly flat or *improves* in most leagues post-2025
(ILT20 +12.5, MLC +13.1, BPL +10.6). The model's own accuracy dropping while
Elo doesn't is the signature of a model that simply never saw 2025/2026
training rows — expected behavior for any fixed-cutoff holdout, present in
nearly every league to some degree.

**2. Extra effect on top — squad-turnover leagues get hit hardest.** The
worst four drops (BBL -34, PSL -33, CPL -23.5, IPL -22.7) are all
**franchise T20 leagues with annual retention-and-auction/trade cycles** —
squads reshuffle heavily between seasons. WPL and SA20, same category,
also show clear drops (-14, -14). By contrast, leagues with stable rosters
— **T20 Blast** (English county cricket, same players year to year) and
**The Hundred** (draft-based but far less churn than an open auction) —
show flat-to-*positive* model accuracy despite the identical training
cutoff. National-team leagues (T20I, WT20I) sit in between: real squad
rotation, but nowhere near a full-league simultaneous reshuffle.

This is the evidence for the squad-turnover feature: it targets exactly
the leagues where the drop is worst, and those leagues cluster precisely
around "annual full-roster market reset" format. It will not close the
whole gap — some of the drop is the fixed-cutoff holdout effect, which no
new feature fixes; that needs the train/val/test split to move forward
(or become per-league / rolling) once enough 2025+ matches exist.

## Follow-up: the gate itself can't see this problem

Trained a date-relative rolling-split model (train to `latest−4mo`,
sigmoid calibration instead of isotonic — isotonic hard-fails the gate
under the smaller rolling val window) and identified the 6 of 28 leagues
whose most recent season falls **entirely** after the new training cutoff
— genuinely never seen in training, not just "recent":

| League | Season | N | Model % | Elo % |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| LPL | 2026 | 21 | 43 | 62 |
| MLC | 2026 | 34 | 50 | 56 |
| T20 Blast | 2026 | 113 | 56 | 59 |
| The Hundred | 2026 | 23 | 43 | 48 |
| The Hundred Women | 2026 | 21 | 43 | 57 |
| Women's T20 Blast | 2026 | 48 | 54 | 58 |

**Model loses to Elo on accuracy in all six.** Pooled global test (n=404):
model 57.7% vs Elo 61.4% accuracy; log-loss 0.654 vs 0.661 (the model's
entire edge is 0.007 nats — smaller than the swing from switching
calibration method alone). `gate_passed: true` under this scheme, same as
the currently-committed model. The gate cannot distinguish "beats Elo" from
"loses to Elo on the metric that actually matters to users" — that's the
real headline finding, ahead of any specific fix.

Note also: BBL 2025/26 (77% under the rolling model), WPL 2025/26 (73%),
IPL 2025 (87%), PSL 2025 (88%) are **not** in the table above — those
seasons fall mostly *before* the new `train_end`, so they're in-sample for
this rolling model, not a real held-out result. Retracting any impression
those numbers show recovery; they don't demonstrate anything about
out-of-sample skill.

Structural finding: only 6 of 28 leagues had a season entirely past a
single global rolling cutoff — franchise leagues run ~2-month annual
bursts that a global date either bisects or fully swallows. A single
global `train_end` cannot produce an honest held-out read for the leagues
this investigation started with (IPL/PSL/BBL/CPL/SA20/WPL). Open design
problem, not solved here.

for the full design this feeds into.
